English phrases that came from these chapters

Some of the passages in this workbook gave English its everyday phrases. A few come straight from the verse; others are just well-known expressions the story inspired.

Part Phrase What it means today
Part 1
“Let there be light”
Genesis 1:3
 Said today when someone suddenly understands something, or when something new begins.
“The breath of life”
Genesis 2:7
 Used for whatever gives someone or something real vitality and energy.
Part 2
“Forbidden fruit”
Genesis 3:6
 Something tempting mainly because it's off-limits.
“Fig leaf”
Genesis 3:7
 A thin, obvious attempt to cover up something embarrassing.
“Adam's apple”
Fun fact · inspired by Genesis 3:6
 The popular English nickname for the bump in the throat. Folklore says a bite of the forbidden fruit stuck in Adam's throat — it's not something the Bible itself says, just a well-known expression the story inspired.
“My brother's keeper”
Genesis 4:9
 Used to ask whether we're responsible for looking after one another.
Part 3
“Forty days and forty nights”
Genesis 7:12
 Used today for any long, difficult stretch of time.
“A flood of …”
Fun fact · inspired by Genesis 7
 Used for an overwhelming rush of something, like “a flood of emails” or “a flood of tears.”
Part 4
“An olive branch”
Genesis 8:11
 To “extend an olive branch” means to offer peace after a conflict.
“A tower of Babel”
Genesis 11:1–9
 Used for a confusing jumble of voices, or a grand plan that collapses into chaos.

Recommended Bible edition

This workbook is keyed to the ESV® Bible (Crossway) — chapter and verse references, proper names and the vocabulary taught all follow the ESV. Read Genesis 1–11 in your own ESV Bible alongside each part; the workbook retells and asks about the passage but does not reprint Scripture text. On the age of the earth, the length of the creation days and the historicity questions around Genesis 1–11, we state what the text says and note plainly where sincere Christians read it differently.
