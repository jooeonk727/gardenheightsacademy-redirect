Noah Builds the Ark
4 Adventures40 min
What Happens in Part 3

Genesis 5 traces a family line from Adam to Noah and Noah's sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth. A repeated pattern records each generation, and Enoch's notice breaks that pattern. In Genesis 6, God sees that human wickedness has become great and that the earth is corrupt and full of violence. Noah finds favor with God. God tells Noah that judgment is coming and gives detailed instructions for an ark. It is to have rooms, three levels, a side door, and pitch inside and outside. God tells Noah to bring his family, animals, and stored food into the ark. Noah does what God commands. In Genesis 7, Noah enters with his wife, his three sons, and their wives. Clean animals and birds come in greater numbers than animals that are not clean. Rain falls for forty days and forty nights. The waters rise, lift the ark, and cover the land described in the account. The chapter closes while the waters are still prevailing.

💎 Treasure Wisdom · a true fact behind the storyMeasuring the Ark in Cubits
Word Study · Ancient MeasurementGenesis gives the ark's length, width, and height in cubits. A cubit was an ancient unit based on the forearm, but its exact length could vary across times and places. Surviving measuring rods show that ancient builders used standard units. The biblical dimensions are 300 cubits long, 50 cubits wide, and 30 cubits high. Those numbers give clear proportions: the length is six times the width and ten times the height. They do not supply one certain conversion into modern feet or meters. We can compare the ark's dimensions with care while keeping an exact modern size approximate.

💎 Treasure Wisdom · a true fact behind the storyCount the Animals Carefully
Close Reading · General and Specific InstructionsGenesis 6 first gives a general instruction: two of every sort of living creature are to be kept alive. Genesis 7 adds a more specific distinction. Clean animals and birds come in greater numbers than animals that are not clean. The chapter names these groups but does not explain the later biblical rules for them. It also uses broad creature groups and the phrase “according to their kinds,” not modern scientific species. Saying “exactly two of every animal” would flatten the two instructions into one. A careful reader keeps the general statement beside the added detail and reads both carefully.

💎 Treasure Wisdom · a true fact behind the storyA Refrain and an Exception
Literary Craft · Repetition and ContrastGenesis 5 is a genealogy, a record that traces a family line. Many entries follow a repeated pattern: a person's age is given, a son is named, other children are mentioned, the full lifespan is counted, and the person dies. The closing notice acts like a refrain. Enoch's entry is an exception. Instead of the usual ending, the account says that he walks with God and that God takes him. Repetition can train readers to expect what comes next. When the pattern changes, the difference receives special attention. The repeated ending and Enoch's different ending create a clear contrast.
Genealogy
A record that traces people in a family line. Genesis 5 follows generations from Adam to Noah and his sons.
Refrain
A line or pattern that returns several times. Many Genesis 5 entries close by reporting the person's death.
Exception
A case that does not follow the usual pattern. Enoch's notice ends differently from the entries around it.

💎 Treasure Wisdom · a true fact behind the storyAncient Boats and Waterproof Coatings
Archaeology · Material EvidenceGenesis calls Noah's vessel an ark and says it is coated with pitch inside and outside. The account gives rooms, three levels, and a side door, but it mentions no sail, oars, or rudder. Archaeologists have found ancient Near Eastern boat remains and models with bitumen, a dark material used to help keep water out. One ancient boat found near Uruk preserved impressions of plant material beneath its coating. Such finds show that waterproof coatings were part of ancient boatbuilding. They help readers picture a material detail in Genesis, but they do not prove the ark's date, place, shape, or construction. Background must stay separate from the passage's direct statements.

Read Genesis 5–7 in your Bible first. Follow the family line until it reaches Noah and his sons. Then notice why judgment is announced, which ark details God gives, and how Noah responds. Keep the general animal instruction in Genesis 6 beside the more specific distinction in Genesis 7. Finally, track the time statements: rain falls for forty days and nights, while the waters prevail for 150 days. Report each detail at the level the passage gives it.

A Flood Story on a Clay Tablet

At the British Museum, a clay tablet from seventh-century BCE Nineveh preserves part of the Epic of Gilgamesh. Its cuneiform writing uses wedge-shaped signs pressed into clay. The story on this tablet includes a flood survivor who reports a warning and the building of a boat. It also mentions family members, animals, birds, and a sacrifice. These details create real similarities with the Genesis flood account, while other details differ, including the gods, the reason for the flood, and the boat's design. The object's date tells when this surviving copy was made, not when the flood it describes occurred. Its existence alone cannot prove that Genesis borrowed from it or that either account happened exactly as described. The tablet is evidence that a flood story was written in ancient Mesopotamia. A careful comparison states that evidence, examines both similarities and differences, and leaves larger conclusions qualified.
